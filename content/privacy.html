1. Who we are and what this covers

QxChat is made by the QxChat team, the people behind the open-source organisation lqxp. You can reach us at contact@qxch.at. Security reports follow /.well-known/security.txt.

This policy covers the QxChat apps (Android from Google Play or as an APK, iOS, desktop, and the web app) and the server software they connect to, LQXP (lqxp/lqxp).

Who is responsible for your data depends on the server you use. QxChat is software anyone can host. Whoever runs the server you connect to is responsible for what that server stores:

  • on your own server, that is you;
  • on a friend's or a community server, that is them;
  • on our public server, qxch.at, that is us, and this policy applies with the default settings.

Because the code is open, someone could run a modified server. Message content stays unreadable even then, because it is encrypted on your device before it is sent. If you want the strongest guarantee, run the server yourself.

2. How we approach your data

  • We ask for as little as possible. There is no email field, no phone number field and no real-name field anywhere in QxChat.
  • We use data only to run the service. Messages, calls, device sync and keeping the service safe from abuse. Never advertising.
  • We never sell data. We do not sell, rent or trade it. The app contains no advertising, analytics, attribution or crash-reporting kit.
  • You can check all of this. The server, the apps and this website are open source under the MIT licence, and the handbook explains how each part works.

6. What we use data for

  • Running accounts, rooms, direct messages, calls, file transfers, device sync, profiles and blocking.
  • Rate limits and anti-abuse checks, with counters that live in memory and build no profile of you.
  • Keeping the service up, with overall load counts kept in memory.

We never use your data for advertising, for building a profile of you, to train AI models, or to sell or give to data brokers.

7. What encryption covers

  • Messages in private rooms are encrypted on your device with AES-256-GCM, using a new key for each message, and signed with ECDSA P-256. The server checks where a message goes and how big it is, never what it says.
  • The server keeps about 150 recent messages per room in memory so people can catch up. They are never written to disk and are lost when the server restarts. There is no message history table.
  • Friend requests (QXP-PHANTOM) and device sync (QxCloudSync) go through relays that cannot open what they carry. Friend requests wait in anonymous slots for 24 hours at most.
  • One exception: a server operator can set up an official announcement room. To send it to everyone, the server holds that room's key, so treat that one room as readable by the operator.

9. Where data is kept, and for how long

  • On your device: a local vault with your session, your keys, recent rooms and messages (about 500 per room) and your settings, plus a longer history of about 2,000 items. With Client Lock on, it is sealed with your PIN (PBKDF2 with 250,000 rounds, then AES-GCM), and secrets in memory are wiped when the app locks.
  • On the server, in memory only, gone on restart: recent room messages, friend request envelopes (24 hours at most), anti-abuse counters, link preview cache (5 minutes) and connection counters.
  • On the server, until you delete them: your account record, sessions, public keys, block tags, your encrypted friend list, rooms, and uploaded files (up to about 10 GB per server), in SQLite or PostgreSQL.
  • Sessions end after 7 days without use. Poll votes are stored as hashes that cannot be linked back to you. If an operator makes backups, how long they keep them is up to them: ask them.

10. Deleting your account and data

  • In the app, on any platform including Android: Settings → Profile → Delete account, confirm, then enter your password. The server deletes your sessions, public keys, block tags, account record, profile picture and banner, and signs out all your devices. The app then clears its local data.
  • Only on this device: Settings → Clear local data removes the local vault and history and leaves your account as it is.
  • Messages and rooms: deleting a message removes it and its attachment. Room owners can delete a whole room, and a room can be set to remove your messages when you leave.
  • By email: write to contact@qxch.at for accounts on qxch.at, with your username. We will ask you to prove the account is yours before deleting anything. For accounts on other servers, contact that server's operator.
  • How fast: deletion in the app is immediate. Messages in server memory are gone at the next restart at the latest. Operator backups, if any, expire on their own schedule. Once your account is deleted, your recovery words no longer work.

11. How we protect it

  • In transit: HTTPS and secure WebSockets in production, with end-to-end encryption on top. TURN credentials are never logged.
  • Stored: passwords and recovery words as Argon2id hashes, session tokens as SHA-256 hashes, no secret stored in plain text, and metadata removed from uploaded images.
  • Against abuse: rate limits, quota tokens, a proof-of-time puzzle and a CAPTCHA, sign-in checks that do not reveal whether a username exists, and link previews that cannot be pointed at internal addresses.
  • What no software can do: protect an unlocked device in someone else's hands, or hide metadata from a server that was modified on purpose. Lock your devices, turn on Client Lock, check download signatures, and host your own server for the most control.

12. Children

QxChat is a general messaging app and is not meant for children under 13, or under 16 where local law sets that age. We do not knowingly collect data from children. If you think a child has given us data, write to contact@qxch.at and to the operator of their server, and we will delete it.

13. Where your data lives

There is no central QxChat cloud. Your data sits where the server you chose is hosted, together with its file storage and TURN relay. Host your own server to choose the country. A call may go through a TURN relay in another region when a direct connection fails, and link previews make the server visit the linked site. For people in the EEA and the UK, transfers rely on encryption, collecting little, and the operator's own safeguards where they apply.

14. Your rights

  • See your data: the app shows your profile and settings; the server returns your stored profile at GET /api/auth/me.
  • Correct it: change your profile and status in the app, and your username once a week.
  • Delete, restrict, object or take a copy: delete your account, messages and rooms (section 10), turn off optional features, and ask your server operator about restriction. There is no automatic export yet: ask your operator, or contact@qxch.at for qxch.at.
  • Start with the operator of your server, since they hold your account. You can also complain to your data protection authority, such as a national authority in the EU or the ICO in the UK, or report the Play version of the app through Google Play.

15. Changes to this policy

Every change is public in our repositories (lqxp/lqxp, lqxp/client, lqxp/site). Important changes are mentioned in the release notes and move the effective date at the top of this page. Using QxChat after that date means you accept the new version. Older versions stay in the git history.
